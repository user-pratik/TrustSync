# TrustSync Known Limitations and Scope

This document details the known technical and operational limitations of the TrustSync perception module as evaluated in Phase 8 and Phase 12.

1. **Video replay defeats lip-sync liveness**: If an attacker plays a video of the legitimate user speaking on a screen facing the robot, the visible lips move in near-perfect synchrony with the audio, yielding a high correlation score. TrustSync specifically defeats audio-only replay attacks (e.g. phone speaker placed beside a still mouth), not full audio-visual screen replay. Mitigations for future work include challenge-response verification (asking the user to repeat a dynamic token), active IR depth illumination, or 3D liveness sensors.
2. **Mimicry**: An adversary who silently mouths along while an external phone speaker broadcasts recorded speech can raise the synchrony score above threshold. The static-mouth check (`min_lip_std`) defends against passive presentation attacks, but not against trained or attentive mimicry.
3. **Correlation chance-level floor**: A mismatched but actively moving mouth (e.g., someone mouthing different words) can still achieve Pearson correlation between 0.30 and 0.50 over short 1.5–2.0 second windows due to shared acoustic-phonetic syllable rates. Consequently, empirical calibration on real speech datasets and static mouth detection are vital safeguards.
4. **Single microphone provides no spatial audio direction**: Audio envelope information is omnidirectional and globally aggregated. If two subjects in frame speak simultaneously, their speech envelopes sum into a single composite acoustic signal, causing attribution ambiguity (`REJECT_AMBIGUOUS` by design).
5. **Generic 3D facial model for head pose**: Head pose is estimated using a standardized 6-point anthropomorphic 3D facial model via `cv2.solvePnP`. Morphological variations across subjects introduce an expected baseline bias of up to 10°, which is mitigated through session-level offset calibration (`tools/calibrate_pose.py`). The camera also serves as the reference proxy for the robot's optical axis.
6. **Small evaluation dataset**: The current benchmark comprises 3 recorded subjects and 64 to 108 clips, resulting in wider statistical confidence intervals. Reporting uses Leave-One-Subject-Out (LOSO) cross-validation and 95% Wilson score intervals to prevent overstating generalization.
7. **Rejection of off-camera live speakers**: Commands spoken by individuals outside the camera's field of view cannot be attributed to a visible mouth, resulting in `REJECT_NO_FACE`. This conservative failure mode prioritizes security over convenience.
8. **Utterance-boundary decision latency**: Final gating decisions are rendered upon utterance termination plus the 0.30 s VAD hangover window. The architecture is engineered for discrete command gating, not instantaneous conversational barge-in.
9. **Privacy considerations**: Because pipeline processing involves high-resolution facial tracking, all video frames and audio streams are processed locally in real-time memory without external network transmission.
